After 65: make room for balance in your training week

A weekly plan can include more than weights and walking. Give balance a clear place without turning it into a risky challenge.

A training week often has two familiar headings: lifting and cardio. For adults aged 65 and older, balance deserves a place too. The CDC's older-adult guidance includes aerobic activity, muscle strengthening and balance. That is a planning framework, not a demand to copy someone else's routine.

Look at the whole week

Start with a blank calendar and name the activities you already do. Walking to the shops, attending a class and training at the gym may occupy different boxes. Now ask a separate question: where does balance appear? An empty box is useful information, not a failed test. It gives you something specific to discuss with a qualified instructor.

Choose support before difficulty

The CDC lists activities such as standing on one leg as balance examples. That does not mean everyone should attempt them unaided. Clear the floor, use a stable support when needed and choose a setting where help is available. Do not turn a balance practice into a competition to use less support.

If you have fallen, feel unsteady or have a condition affecting movement, talk to a health professional about suitable activities. A physical therapist or qualified instructor can help with positioning and support. This article cannot determine which exercise is safe for you.

Give each part a clear job

  • Aerobic activity: keep the walking, cycling or other activity that fits your abilities.
  • Strength work: retain a place for the major muscle groups rather than replacing every session with balance practice.
  • Balance: identify an appropriate activity and an opportunity to practise it without rushing.

The CDC's activity examples explain these different roles. Your practical next step can simply be asking whether a class includes balance and what adaptations it offers.

Review the setup, not a score

At the end of the week, write down which activities you completed and whether the setting felt manageable. Record questions for your next class instead of inventing a pass mark. A longer unsupported hold is not automatically the right goal. The plan should remain understandable and repeatable, with room to change it when your circumstances change.
